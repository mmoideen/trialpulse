## 2. Aggregate Adverse Events by System Organ Class
<!-- page: 2 -->

This section presents aggregate adverse event counts across the revarafusp alfa program, coded to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) System Organ Class (SOC) level, for the second quarter of 2026. Skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders were reported in 218 events during Q2 2026. This is consistent with the known association between EGFR-targeted therapy and dermatologic toxicity, with acneiform dermatitis, dry skin, and paronychia the most frequently reported preferred terms within this SOC. Gastrointestinal disorders were reported in 141 events during Q2 2026. The most common preferred terms were diarrhea, nausea, and stomatitis, the majority of which were Grade 1 or Grade 2 in severity and managed with supportive care. Metabolism and nutrition disorders were reported in 96 events during Q2 2026. This category was dominated by hypomagnesemia and hypokalemia, consistent with the expected safety profile described in the current Investigator Brochure. Vascular disorders were reported in 47 events during Q2 2026. Hypertension was the predominant preferred term, consistent with the signal evaluation summarized in the 2026 Development Safety Update Report. Aggregate counts in this section reflect events regardless of causality assessment and do not include participant-level identifying information.

## 3. Serious Adverse Events
<!-- page: 5 -->

This section summarizes serious adverse events (SAEs) reported across the revarafusp alfa program during the second quarter of 2026. There were 34 serious adverse events reported in Q2 2026. These SAEs span both AXIOM-HN01 and AXIOM-HN02, with disease progression and infection the most frequently reported SAE categories overall. Six serious adverse events were assessed as related to revarafusp alfa. The investigator-assessed related events comprised two cases of Grade 4 infusion-related reaction, two cases of Grade 3 acneiform dermatitis with secondary infection, one case of Grade 3 hypertension, and one case of Grade 4 pneumonitis, the latter of which was also reported as a SUSAR and is detailed further in the SUSAR Line Listing for this quarter. There were no treatment-related deaths in Q2 2026. All SAEs assessed as related to study treatment were reviewed by the sponsor's safety physician and reported to participating regulatory authorities within applicable expedited reporting timelines.

## 4. Narrative Summaries
<!-- page: 8 -->

This section provides brief narrative summaries for the serious adverse events assessed as related to study treatment during the second quarter of 2026, prepared to support signal evaluation and Data Monitoring Committee review. Participant 014-0032, a 61-year-old male, experienced Grade 4 infusion-related reaction on Cycle 1 Day 1. The infusion was stopped immediately upon onset of symptoms, the participant was treated with intravenous corticosteroids and antihistamines, and the event resolved within 24 hours without sequelae; the participant was withdrawn from study treatment per protocol following a Grade 4 infusion-related reaction. Participant 027-0011, initials J.M., experienced Grade 3 hypertension requiring dose interruption. Blood pressure was managed with the addition of a second antihypertensive agent, and study treatment was resumed at the same dose after blood pressure returned to Grade 1 or lower for two consecutive assessments. Both events were reported to the site's institutional review board and to applicable regulatory authorities within expedited reporting timelines. These narratives contain the minimum participant-level detail required for safety signal evaluation and are not intended for aggregate reporting purposes.
