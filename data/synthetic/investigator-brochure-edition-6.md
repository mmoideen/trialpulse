## 2. Mechanism of Action
<!-- page: 4 -->

Revarafusp alfa is a bifunctional antibody targeting EGFR and TGF-beta. It is designed to simultaneously block a well-established oncogenic driver pathway in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and to neutralize a key mediator of tumor-associated immune suppression. The EGFR arm is a high-affinity IgG1 and the TGF-beta arm is a ligand trap. The ligand trap binds and sequesters all three TGF-beta isoforms (TGF-beta1, TGF-beta2, and TGF-beta3) in the tumor microenvironment, preventing them from engaging TGF-beta receptors on infiltrating immune cells. By combining EGFR blockade with local TGF-beta neutralization in a single molecule, revarafusp alfa is designed to reduce systemic TGF-beta-related toxicity relative to a systemic small-molecule TGF-beta receptor inhibitor, while concentrating TGF-beta trapping activity at sites of EGFR overexpression, which is common in HNSCC tumor tissue. Preclinical models suggest that this dual mechanism enhances anti-tumor T-cell infiltration and activity when combined with checkpoint inhibitors such as torilizumab, providing the scientific rationale for the AXIOM-HN01 and AXIOM-HN02 combination studies.

## 5. Reference Safety Information
<!-- page: 19 -->

This section presents the reference safety information for revarafusp alfa as of Edition 6, updated following additional exposure accrued across AXIOM-HN01 and AXIOM-HN02 and summarized in the 2026 Development Safety Update Report. Edition 6 adds Grade 3 or higher hypertension as an identified risk. This follows signal evaluation that identified a numerically higher rate of hypertension in revarafusp alfa-treated participants relative to background rates in this population, consistent with on-target TGF-beta pathway inhibition, which is mechanistically linked to blood pressure regulation. Edition 6 revises Grade 3 or higher acneiform dermatitis incidence to 13 percent. This reflects the larger, more mature safety database now available compared with Edition 5. Infusion-related reactions remain an identified risk requiring premedication. Extended post-infusion observation, described in the current protocol, further mitigates this risk. Hypomagnesemia remains an expected adverse reaction and continues to be manageable with supplementation. Investigators should review Section 7 for updated dose modification guidance corresponding to these safety updates.

## 7. Guidance for the Investigator
<!-- page: 27 -->

This section provides practical dose modification and monitoring guidance for investigators managing participants receiving revarafusp alfa. Investigators must hold dosing for Grade 3 acneiform dermatitis until recovery to Grade 1 or lower. Dosing may then resume at the same or a reduced dose per the toxicity management table in Appendix C; a second occurrence of Grade 3 acneiform dermatitis after dose reduction should prompt discontinuation of revarafusp alfa. Magnesium levels should be monitored every cycle. Monitoring should occur prior to dosing, given the identified risk of hypomagnesemia, with supplementation initiated per institutional practice for any participant with a serum magnesium level below the lower limit of normal. Blood pressure should be assessed at every visit given the newly identified hypertension risk described in Section 5, with dose interruption considered for Grade 3 hypertension that does not respond to standard antihypertensive management within one week.
