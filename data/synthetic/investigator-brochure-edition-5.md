## 2. Mechanism of Action
<!-- page: 4 -->

Revarafusp alfa is a bifunctional antibody targeting EGFR and TGF-beta. It is designed to simultaneously block a well-established oncogenic driver pathway in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and to neutralize a key mediator of tumor-associated immune suppression. The EGFR arm is a high-affinity IgG1 and the TGF-beta arm is a ligand trap. The ligand trap binds and sequesters all three TGF-beta isoforms (TGF-beta1, TGF-beta2, and TGF-beta3) in the tumor microenvironment, preventing them from engaging TGF-beta receptors on infiltrating immune cells. By combining EGFR blockade with local TGF-beta neutralization in a single molecule, revarafusp alfa is designed to reduce systemic TGF-beta-related toxicity relative to a systemic small-molecule TGF-beta receptor inhibitor, while concentrating TGF-beta trapping activity at sites of EGFR overexpression, which is common in HNSCC tumor tissue. Preclinical models suggest that this dual mechanism enhances anti-tumor T-cell infiltration and activity when combined with checkpoint inhibitors such as torilizumab, providing the scientific rationale for the AXIOM-HN01 and AXIOM-HN02 combination studies.

## 5. Reference Safety Information
<!-- page: 18 -->

This section presents the reference safety information for revarafusp alfa as of Edition 5, reflecting cumulative safety data from the completed Phase 1 dose-escalation study and ongoing Phase 1b expansion cohorts. The expected adverse reactions include acneiform dermatitis, infusion-related reactions, and hypomagnesemia. These are consistent with the known class effects of EGFR-targeted therapy combined with the infusion characteristics typical of a bifunctional biologic. Edition 5 lists Grade 3 or higher acneiform dermatitis at an incidence of 11 percent. This reflects the pooled Phase 1 safety population, with onset typically occurring within the first two treatment cycles and generally manageable with topical and oral antibiotic therapy, dose interruption, or dose reduction per the toxicity management algorithm in Appendix C. Infusion-related reactions occurred predominantly with the first or second infusion and were manageable with premedication and, where needed, temporary interruption of the infusion. Hypomagnesemia was generally low-grade and responsive to oral or intravenous magnesium supplementation without requiring treatment discontinuation.
